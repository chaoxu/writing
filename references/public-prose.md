# Public Prose

This guide covers essays, posts, emails, project descriptions, documentation, applications, bios, and announcements.

## Build the piece around a position

Identify the sentence the piece depends on. State it without a reflexive hedge. Each following paragraph should supply evidence, a consequence, a limitation, or a transition to the next claim.

Prefer one specific practitioner's detail over a generic claim of importance. “The browser returned 502 while curl worked” carries more information than “network reliability is crucial.”

Describe function rather than revision history. Documentation says what a system does. Commit messages and release notes say what changed.

## Openings

Begin with the subject, event, or decision.

- Weak: “It is worth noting that our paper has now officially been published.”
- Direct: “Our paper is now published in ESA 2026.”
- Weak: “面对强模型，外部系统应该做多少事？Elenx 的答案是……”
- Direct: “Elenx 把数学思考尽量留在模型内部，因为模型的推理能力很强。”

An essay may begin with a concrete episode before stating the larger position. The episode must do argumentative work, not function as a cinematic hook.

## Explanations and arguments

Name the mechanism. Replace aphorisms with the fact they gesture at. “Verification is the bottleneck” should say which claims fail verification and where.

Use contrast only when both sides matter. Avoid repeated “not X but Y” constructions. State Y directly, then preserve one necessary exclusion if it defines the boundary.

Mixed Chinese and English terminology is acceptable when it reflects how practitioners speak. Define an English term once if the audience may not know it.

## Emails and short messages

Put the request, decision, or news in the first sentence. Include the context needed to act. End when the recipient knows what to do.

Skip pleasantries that exist only to soften an otherwise normal request. Keep genuine thanks, congratulations, sympathy, and relationship-specific warmth.

## Essays and posts

Use headings when the argument makes a real turn. A heading names the content of the section, not its rhetorical role. Avoid headings such as “Key Takeaways,” “Why This Matters,” or “Looking Ahead” unless those phrases name the actual subject.

Let the author's uncertainty remain visible where it is part of the position. A concrete admission such as “我自己也没底” is stronger than generalized caution. Do not add uncertainty to claims the author presents as settled.

Close on the final substantive claim. Do not append a recap, inspirational slogan, invitation to “dive deeper,” or manufactured punchline.
