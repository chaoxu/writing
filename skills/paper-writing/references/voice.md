# Voice

## Lead with the claim

Open with an affirmative declarative sentence. State what the subject is, does, or causes before describing boundaries or rejected alternatives.

Do not manufacture a rhetorical question to introduce its own answer or correct an objection the reader never made. When a paragraph piles up denials, rewrite it around the claim it advances. Keep negation when the exclusion is an exact fact, logical condition, capability boundary, or correction grounded in the source.

## Delete empty metadiscourse

Apply the deletion test: remove the phrase. If the claim and its logic survive, leave it removed.

Common English deletions include “to be honest,” “frankly,” “simply put,” “basically,” “essentially,” “actually,” “in fact,” “it is worth noting,” “importantly,” “interestingly,” “as we all know,” “at the end of the day,” and “in other words” when the previous wording was already clear.

Common Chinese deletions include 说白了、老实说、坦率地讲、讲真、简单来说、简而言之、总的来说、综上所述、值得注意的是、需要指出的是、有意思的是、更重要的是、不难看出、我们可以看到、其实、事实上、众所周知, and redundant 在我看来.

Keep 当然、不过、但是, “however,” and similar words when they carry a real concession or contrast. These are contextual judgments, not mechanical word bans. Mathematical uses such as “essentially bounded” keep their technical meaning.

## Let content carry voice

Use concrete details supported by the material. Preserve the author's uncertainty where it is part of the position. Add neither reflexive hedges nor unsupported confidence. Words such as “genuine,” “truly,” “real,” and 确实 need a substantive contrast to earn their place.

Prefer verbs over nominalizations: “verify” over “perform verification,” 验证 over 进行验证. Copulas are fine. Prefer “is” and “has” over “serves as,” “boasts,” or “leverages.”

Replace aphorisms and trailing significance claims with the specific mechanism or evidence. Concrete details should come from the source, not from attempts to simulate personality.

## Rhythm and punctuation

Let sentence length follow the reasoning. A long logical chain can end with a short declarative consequence. Avoid manufactured punchlines, dramatic fragments, and line breaks used only for emphasis.

List the number of items the content contains. Avoid decorative triples and false ranges such as “from X to Y” when X and Y are merely examples. Headings and boldface should help navigation or emphasis.

Authorial prose does not use semicolons. Split independent claims or use a colon for an explanation. Citation syntax, formal notation, and quoted text may retain semicolons. Use em dashes sparingly, at most one pair per sentence. Avoid the middle dot as a decorative prose separator.

Keep numbers that carry a claim, measurement, provenance, or actionable count. Remove counts and numeric symmetry used only to decorate a heading or imply significance.

## Terminology

Use established and defined technical names consistently. Once a mathematical concept has a name, keep that name rather than inventing synonyms for variety. Preserve different names for different concepts. Review a new or sparse term for the distinction it contributes, and replace redundant names with the established term. The verifier explains why a suspected alias denotes the same concept before recommending a change. Vocabulary counts are review aids, not a target score.

## Register

Preserve the user's language and degree of formality. Mixed Chinese and established English terminology is acceptable when natural for the audience. Define unfamiliar technical terms and avoid forced slang.

Write for an intelligent, cooperative reader. Keep genuine thanks, congratulations, sympathy, and relationship-specific warmth. Remove generic pleasantries and unsolicited closing offers. End when the substantive work of the passage is complete.
