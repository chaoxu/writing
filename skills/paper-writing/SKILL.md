# Paper Writing

Write a manuscript with a clear result, an auditable argument, and exact attribution. Preserve the user's hypotheses, targets, uncertainty, and requested level of generality. A partial result remains partial.

**Cite established results instead of proving them again.** Check that the source covers the exact claim. For a routine adaptation, cite the source and prove only the necessary difference. Separate imported ingredients from a new synthesis or argument.

Read [references/mathematics.md](references/mathematics.md) for structure, proofs, notation, and attribution, and [references/voice.md](references/voice.md) for prose. Both are bundled here. Follow the manuscript's existing format and any applicable authoring rules. User instructions take precedence over these style defaults.

## Draft or restructure

1. Identify the main result, its assumptions, the available proof, the audience, and the intended format. Resolve material mathematical ambiguities before writing around them.
2. For substantive mathematical work, keep a compact working ledger of known results, routine adaptations, syntheses, and new arguments. Verify the primary-source passages supporting the claims being written.
3. Organize around one coherent scope. Present the problem, relevant prior work, the result, and the key proof idea. Introduce technical machinery where the reader needs it.
4. Write the proof at that scope, citing standard machinery and supplying the new steps. Remove duplicate explanations and unnecessary vocabulary while preserving load-bearing details.

For an existing manuscript, preserve deliberate author wording and proof ideas unless the requested restructuring covers them. Keep a recoverable version before major rearrangement. A restructuring request authorizes that work. Ask only when a material scope or mathematical choice remains unresolved.

## Check the deliverable

Check the affected mathematical implications, attribution, definitions, notation, cross-references, bibliography, and build or rendering. Combine checks when practical. A compilation proves that the document builds, not that its mathematics is correct. Inspect layout when it matters to the request. If a tool or source is unavailable, identify the unfinished check without claiming it passed.

Separate reviewers are useful only when the task warrants them and delegation is available and authorized. Repeat checks after relevant changes, failures, or unresolved findings.

Deliver the manuscript in the requested format. Briefly report substantive limitations, unresolved proof or citation questions, and the validation actually performed. Writing a result does not certify its novelty.
